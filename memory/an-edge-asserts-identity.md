The map is a graph: a node is a term with its definition, an edge is a tappable link.
**An edge asserts an identity — "this word here IS the catalogued item at the other end."**
So an edge can be false in a way a definition cannot, and it fails silently: it looks like
helpful navigation and teaches a relation the tradition does not make.

## The test before drawing one

**Compare the two glossary entries' genus.** If the genus differs, it is almost certainly
not the same item, however identical the English looks. Two edges shipped on this map and
had to be pulled, both caught this way:

- **"inappropriate attention" → the Attention factor.** Wrong. The glossary calls
  **Attention** *"a mental factor"* that focuses the mind on an attribute and accompanies
  every cognition; it calls **Inappropriate attention** *"a mind"* that exaggerates the
  qualities of a contaminated object and generates delusions. The bare factor accompanies
  that cognition — but **accompaniment is not identity.** *Inappropriate attention* is its
  own headword, which is the real destination.
- **"distraction" (a cause of delusion) → the Distraction secondary delusion.** Wrong. The
  cause is the compound *"distraction and being influenced by others"* — bad company,
  bustle, misleading talk — a **circumstance**. The secondary delusion is an internal
  mental factor that wanders to objects of delusion. A compressed label on the page
  (*"distraction & bad influence"*) hid the compound and invited the error.

**The reasoning that produced both mistakes was: "the map's own structure supports it."**
The Attention card lists *appropriate / inappropriate* as one of its divisions, so linking
looked consistent with the page. But the page is the thing being checked. **Consistency
with our own layout is not evidence about the tradition** — see
[[terms-follow-the-official-glossary]], which says the same about wording. Check the
glossary, and check whether the phrase is a headword in its own right before pointing its
last word somewhere else.

## What a legitimate edge looks like

**Identity with qualification is fine.** The *power of aspiration* that supports effort is
the object-ascertaining factor **aspiration** with a virtuous object. A power of aspiration
*is* an aspiration; the converse does not hold, but **a link to a general class node does
not assert the converse**, so the edge is sound. Śāntideva has *chanda* there, the same
term as the factor — though the Tibetan *mos pa* lets a commentator foreground conviction
instead, which is why this one is high confidence rather than certain.

**A member list is always a legitimate edge** and is the highest-value linking on the map:
a note that enumerates its members should walk to each of them. The six group notes, the
five similarities and the scope-practitioner meditations were all dead ends until linked.

## Near misses worth remembering

- **Relaxation is not Mental suppleness.** *dor ba*, taking a timely rest to sustain
  effort, versus *praśrabdhi*, flexibility of mind induced by virtuous concentration.
- **Steadfastness must never point at Deluded pride**, though the Sanskrit *māna* in the
  following verse invites exactly that. Its confidence is courage about completing virtue;
  deluded pride exaggerates one's own qualities.
- **"Object" among the causes of delusion is not the Object similarity.** That node means
  a primary mind and its factors observe the *same* object; the cause is an attractive or
  unattractive object met in the world. Related in the broadest sense, not identical.
- **Do not link the ubiquitous genus terms** — *mind*, *object*, *cognizer*, *mental
  factor*, *primary mind*. *Cognizer* is the genus of roughly twenty definitions; linking
  every occurrence buries the notes and teaches nothing. Link the specific, skip the
  universal.
- **Never link an adjective to a group node.** Every prose occurrence of *virtuous* on
  this map is the plain adjective (*virtuous object*, *non-virtuous*); not one means the
  group of eleven virtuous factors.

## Counting hazard

**Never count a term's mentions across a whole note entry.** The `kind:` field renders as
the note's category subtitle — plain text that cannot hold a link — so counting it inflates
every figure. *Delusion* looked like it was named in 31 notes; in prose it is 8, the rest
being the labels *"Root delusion"* and *"Secondary delusion"*. *Virtuous* looked like 19
and is really 0. **Search `def:` and `ex:` only.**

**Why:** a wrong edge is the exact failure this collection cannot catch later — confident,
plausible, invisible on the page, and indistinguishable from correct navigation. It is also
the failure the maintainer is least able to spot, because it looks like the map working.

**How to apply:** before drawing an edge, read both glossary entries and compare their
genus; ask whether the phrase is a headword of its own; and when the answer turns on
doctrine rather than editing, ask [[consulting-dharma-codex]] instead of deciding. Settled
2026-09-10, after two false edges had already shipped.
