A

Nature & Levels of Mind

What mind is, and its broadest divisions

Definition: that which is clarity and knowing. Its nature is empty like space, always lacking form, shape and colour; its function is to perceive or know objects.

Classified by level

  • 1 — waking, sense-based
  • 2 — for example, dreaming
  • 3 — deep sleep, death, clear light

Classified by function

  • 1 — apprehend the mere entity
  • 2 — apprehend particular attributes

The engine of section B below.

Synonyms, and the line that divides them

Six words circle this territory. Three name one thing; three are wider. Treating all six as interchangeable is the easy mistake.

primary mind consciousness mentality

“Primary mind, consciousness and mentality are synonymous terms.”

The book states this three times — in Part One, in Part Two with a separate Tibetan word set beside each (rnam shes for consciousness, yid for mentality), and again in Appendix 1: three words, one referent. The Kadampa Glossary says it in the same breath.

Consciousness is the word used when they are counted off — eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mental. Mentality is far rarer, and surfaces where a primary mind is doing duty as the basis for the next moment of knowing: “it is a mentality because it is a primary mind.”

mind awareness cognizer

Wider, and not interchangeable with the three above — each of these takes in the mental factors too. Mind names the nature: clarity and knowing. Awareness sorts that same field by what it depends on, giving sense awareness and mental awareness. Cognizer is the word the definitions themselves use, which is why every definition on this page opens with it.

“The sense consciousnesses are primary minds. Their retinue of secondary minds, or mental factors, are sense awarenesses, but not consciousnesses.”

So a mental factor is a mind, an awareness and a cognizer, and never a consciousness. That sentence is what holds the two groups apart — and why this is two lists rather than one.

Quoted from Cómo comprender la mente (Editorial Tharpa, 2014) — Part One, Part Two and Appendix 1 — rendered into the glossary's English.

The 51 factors fall into 6 groups →

5

Minimum conditions present in every cognition.

  1. experiences the object as pleasant / unpleasant / neutral · physical / mental · contaminated / uncontaminated
  2. apprehends the distinguishing characteristic · six, by consciousness · correct / erroneous · clear / confused
  3. directs the primary mind to the object; creates karma · harmful / virtuous / neutral · meritorious / non-meritorious / non-fluctuating
  4. contacts the object; basis of feeling · six, by consciousness
  5. focuses on a particular feature · correct / incorrect · appropriate / inappropriate
5

Stabilise & qualify the grip on the object — the ladder of learning & meditation.

  1. seeks a desired object: to meet / not part from / obtain / be free from · correct / incorrect
  2. holds the object firmly · correct / incorrect
  3. does not forget the object (memory) · new / old · with / without sinking & excitement
  4. stays one-pointedly · 9 levels by realm (desire; 4 form; infinite space, infinite consciousness, nothingness, peak of samsara) · · mundane / supramundane · conventional / ultimate object
  5. realises a meaningful object · from hearing / contemplation / meditation · 7 forms: great, clear, quick, profound, expounding Dharma, debate, composing texts
11

Naturally virtuous — causes of peace & the antidotes of the path.

  1. believing · admiring · wishing
  2. restraint of body / speech / mind for one’s own sake
  3. restraint of body / speech / mind for others’ sake
  4. to places / enjoyments / bodies of samsara · to this life / samsara / solitary peace
  5. toward harmful beings / painful things / resulting suffering
  6. from hearing / contemplation / meditation / imprints
  7. armour · non-discouragement · application · non-satisfaction — with : aspiration, steadfastness, joy, relaxation
  8. subtle / gross — induced by concentration
  9. virtuous root of mundane / supramundane paths
  10. coarse-effort / subtle-effort / effortless
  11. compassion: wishing beings free from suffering / from its causes · mere / superior
6

Roots of all disturbance & suffering.

  1. to past / present / future objects · of the desire / form / formless realm · to places / enjoyments / bodies of samsara
  2. 9 ways of arising: past / present / future harm — to oneself, to loved ones, or help to enemies
  3. 7 kinds: over inferiors, equals, superiors; pride of identity, presumptuous, emulative, wrong pride
  4. of conventional / ultimate truths · of karma / of emptiness
  5. leaning toward truth / away from truth / balanced
  6. 5 kinds: · extreme view · wrong views held as supreme · wrong morality/conduct held as supreme · wrong view

Classified by realm · by cause (innate / intellectually acquired) · by entity (root / secondary). Each delusion spans 9 realms × 9 grades = 81 degrees.

20

Derived from & intensifying the root delusions.

4

Virtuous, non-virtuous or neutral depending on context.

  1. arises from dullness; absorbs the sense awarenesses
  2. over actions done in the past
  3. examines an object’s gross nature
  4. examines an object’s subtle nature

Ways Minds Know Objects

Epistemology — how knowing happens, and when it is reliable

Conceptual vs nonconceptual mind

Apprehends its object by means of a (the appearing object of a conceptual mind).

Five object-relations:

  • — how it appears
  • — what is looked at
  • — what it is held to
  • — what is taken in
  • — what is imagined

Three origins: hearing / reading · contemplating the meaning · previous imprints. Correct or mistaken.

Its object appears clearly, unmixed with a generic image.

Two main classes:

  • · sense awarenesses
  • · nonconceptual mental awarenesses

Three nonconceptual mental awarenesses:

  • · direct mental perceivers
  • · yogic direct perceivers
  • · other nonconceptual mental awarenesses

May also be correct or mistaken.

Sense vs mental awareness

Generated from a form sense power. Five: eye · ear · nose · tongue · body.

Dominant condition — common / uncommon.

Generated from a mental power — the mentality that directly produces its particular aspect.

Conceptual / nonconceptual · virtuous / non-virtuous / neutral.
